Free drinking water in Rome: how to find nasoni fountains

Rome has thousands of public drinking fountains, often called nasoni. They are free, useful in summer, and perfect for refilling your bottle while exploring the city.

 Download Fontanella

What are nasoni?

Nasoni are Rome’s public drinking fountains. The word comes from the fountain’s curved metal spout, which looks a little like a large nose.

You can find them on streets, near squares, close to parks, and around many popular walking routes. They are one of the simplest ways to refill a bottle while visiting Rome.

Quick tip: If water is constantly flowing from a public fountain in Rome, it is usually meant for drinking unless there is a sign saying otherwise.

Why tourists use them

Rome can get very hot, especially in late spring and summer. Buying bottled water again and again can be annoying, expensive, and wasteful.

Nasoni are useful because they let you:

  • refill your bottle for free
  • stay hydrated while walking around Rome
  • avoid carrying too much water at once
  • reduce single-use plastic bottles
  • find water near tourist areas, parks, and local streets

How to refill your bottle

Most nasoni are simple to use. Place your bottle under the spout and fill it directly. Some fountains also let you block the main spout with a finger so water comes out from a small hole on top, making it easier to drink from.

Frequently asked questions

Is fountain water in Rome free?

Yes, public drinking fountains in Rome are free to use.

Are nasoni only in Rome?

The word “nasoni” is strongly associated with Rome. Other Italian cities also have public drinking fountains, but they may use different local names.
